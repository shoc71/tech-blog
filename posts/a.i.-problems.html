Myth of Automation

The biggest and most seductive lie about A.I. / automation is our work can be done without the need of human intervention/interaction.

It's the dream we all like to believe in one way or another but the truth is there is no such thing as a perpetual machine. Every physics major student should be able to tell you why this perpetual machine are not perpetual (1. Inevitable loss of unusable heat from a system either through fiction / burning / air resistance - 2. No way to create energy nor destroy it.)

Yeah but no one is creating a perpetual motion machines and that's not what automation means. Two things, 1. I wish to break the Laws of Thermodynamics and 2. everybody wants a perpetual machine but we call them A.I. overlords or automation.

It's a strange timeline we live in, half of us are scared to death by A.I. and "fighting" against the production and training of that A.I. while the other half is fueling the production of A.I. for their nefarious gains and to fuel their own laziness. Everyone who thinks only about the bottom-line will care only about the quantity and the A.I. can do that for you in droves.

Data and it's many issues

The biggest question that most of the A.I. tech bros are trying to answer is, what information is junk? what information is quality data? Which one get's a higher priority over others Do we have enough data to give an educated guess about this?

The way this will be solved is by creating checkpoints/back-ups for the A.I. Having all the information stored for the millionth time on a database server in the middle of the homeland of China, and continously adding and re-fining what data points are good and what's trash.

This requires constant human overwatch to make sure the A.I. doesn't steer in the wrong direction (assuming the overlords care) AND/OR the constant filtering of data for the A.I. to maintain it's intelligence and not over-correct/get it wrong entirely.

I assume the success rate for the type of content it produces varies, however, on average it must have a success rate of >95% (pulled this number out my ass), in order, for the greater mass to continously use and pay for it.

I genuinely believe that a lot of the jobs related to AI will be mass-filtering job, data creation for the AI, constant testing to make sure it doesn't break.

It was designed by human developers (who know what they are doing) for humans consumers (who don't know what they want).
